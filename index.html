🌾 Will food get more expensive?

Food costs 7.74% more than a year ago (September; the next reading is due 12 November), with onions more than doubled. That is just under the painful mark of 8%. The short monsoon in the south and east reaches the shops in November, December and January. Fertiliser stocks are 38% above last year, so the March and April harvest looks protected. Buy winter staples this month.

Fertiliser shippingRoute shut — stocks ample

A third of the world’s fertiliser ships through one stretch of sea near Iran. It is still closed. A vessel was struck there on 1 November. On 2 November the US and Iran both signalled room for talks; Iran says nothing is agreed.

Imported DAP is costing about $910–930 a tonne, far above what the capped bag price covers, so the government pays importers extra. India started winter sowing with fertiliser stocks 38% above last year (mid-October), so the March and April harvest looks less exposed.

💰 What about money?

The cost of living is 5.54% higher than a year ago, against 1.54% last September; the next reading is due 12 November. What shops pay suppliers is rising at 11.39%, and that gap closes by shop prices rising. The rupee holds near ₹94.86 to the dollar, but only with heavy spending of savings. The Reserve Bank raised its rate to 5.50% on 7 October. Hold a larger cash reserve and postpone commitments you cannot easily undo.

🔍 Indicators of what is to come

These turn before shop prices do. Two of the five main readings are severe: Pacific warming at +2.5°C and a monsoon 13% short. Oil eased to about $106 on talk of US–Iran negotiations, but nothing is agreed. What shops pay suppliers has jumped to 11.39%.

The buffers keep thinning. Savings fell for a seventh week to $719.75 billion, while promised future dollar sales reached a record $239.2 billion at the end of September.

What shops pay suppliersTwice your rate

Moves two to three months before you feel it, so this reaches the shops in December and January. No new figure until mid-November. September’s figure, out 14 October, is 11.39% against 5.54% in the shops, with fuel and power up 25.9%. A year ago it was 0.13%. That gap closes by shop prices climbing, not the other way round.

Foreign-currency savingsFalling seven weeks

India’s rainy-day fund, spent to stop the rupee falling. It fell for a seventh week to $719.75 billion on 23 October, about $66 billion below the 4 September record.

Against it, the Reserve Bank has promised away a record $239.2 billion in future dollar sales, leaving roughly $480 billion genuinely free. That promise figure is from 30 September, three weeks older than the savings figure, so treat the free amount as approximate.

Room to cushion pricesAlready spent

Governments soften price shocks by cutting taxes. Diesel tax is already at zero, so there’s nothing left to cut for this one. Either pump prices rise or government borrowing does — both reach you.

Interest ratesRaised on 7 Oct

The rate that sets your loan payments. On 7 October the Reserve Bank raised it to 5.50%, the first rise since February 2023, and said it will tighten carefully. It now expects the cost of living to average 5.2% through March 2027. A cut looks unlikely. If you have a loan, plan on it costing more.

🌫 Is the air safe?

Readings eased to about 60 in the first days of November, in the fine range, against 168 on a private tracker on 15 October; trackers disagree. Smog usually climbs after Diwali on 8 November and through the winter cold. Run an air purifier in the elders’ room now, and keep the elderly indoors on days above 300.
